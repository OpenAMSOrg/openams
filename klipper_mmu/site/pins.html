klipper-mmu docs

Your MCU and your pins  /  configuration, chip first

Your board, your pins.

You configure klipper-mmu in Klipper's own make menuconfig. Choose the chip first, then the unit, then the pins. A board preset fills in default pins; you can change every one of them.

1. The chip

Open the menu in your build checkout (see the quick start):

make KCONFIG_CONFIG="$PWD/boxturtle.config" OUT=out-boxturtle/ menuconfig

The first screens are Klipper's: architecture (STM32), processor model, bootloader offset, clock and USB or CAN. On an AFC Lite that is an STM32H723. If a chip lacks something an option needs, such as ADC inputs for an analog buffer, the menu hides the option or the build refuses it with a message.

2. The unit

Enable klipper-mmu, then open Device, board and lanes:

  • MMU Device Composition: your unit (Supported units), or Custom.
  • Wiring preset (all pins editable): AFC Lite (H723 only), or Manual pin configuration to start empty.
  • Lane count override: for units that come in more than one size, such as a 4- or 8-lane BoxTurtle.

The device sets the selector kind, sensors and motion defaults. Mechanism and optional feedback lets you change them: separate LOAD and BAY sensors, powered rewind, a filament encoder, an analog buffer, and how a load detects arrival.

3. The pins

Open Configure and reserve MMU pins. Pins use their datasheet names (PA1, PC12). Per-lane signals take a comma list in lane order:

PD4,PC12,PE2,PD15
Reserved pins

These pins now belong to the unit. Do not use them again in printer.cfg.

Sensors, buffer, encoder and respoolers

Under Presence, buffer pins and electrical settings and Rewind pins and bridge settings:

Menu entryWhat it is
Bay/preparation sensor pins, in lane orderThe switch at each lane's entrance. Clicks when you insert filament.

Each switch has an active level and a pull, under Local switch electrical settings:

SettingValuesSame as in printer.cfg
Active level0 = low, 1 = high means “filament here”! in front of a pin
Pull-1 = down, 0 = none, 1 = up^ for a pull-up

You do not need to get these right first time. The switch check names any switch that is reversed or floating.

An analog buffer (the OpenAMS FPS) needs Paired analog DiffA/DiffB buffer on and two ADC-capable pins; then calibrate it. A switched buffer needs Buffer input that requests forward feeding set to the switch reached when the buffer wants more filament.

If a spool unwinds when it should wind, swap that lane's two rewind drive pins.

4. Mechanics

Under Motion and driver defaults, describe the feeder as Klipper describes a stepper. The build turns it into steps.

Menu entryMeaningBoxTurtle default
Feeder rotation distance (micrometres per output rotation)Filament moved by one turn of the drive gear22000 (22 mm)
Feeder motor full steps per rotation200 for a 1.8° motor, 400 for 0.9°200
Feeder gear ratio: motor turns (A) / output turns (B)A 50:10 gearbox is A = 50, B = 1050 : 10
Driver run-current default (mA)From your motor's datasheetDevice default
Driver hold-current default (mA)Current at restDevice default

Each device fills in its own values from Happy Hare's or AFC's configuration for that unit. To measure rotation distance yourself, mark the filament, turn the drive gear exactly one turn by hand, and measure what went in.

Selector units have the same entries for the selector axis under Selector stepper axis mechanics, and their gate geometry under the selector menus. The same menus hold speeds, acceleration and deadlines; change them one at a time, after the unit loads reliably.

5. Build, flash, check

Save, then build and flash as in the quick start (step 2, step 3). Your settings stay in your config file for the next build.

Before motors get power

A wrong pin can drive the wrong motor. After any pin change, check switches and the buffer with the safe image before flashing the motion image. Then run commissioning again.

klipper-mmu is an optional add-on to Klipper, a separate project with its own authors and license (GPL-3.0). It builds on the work of the Happy Hare, AFC, BoxTurtle and OpenAMS projects.
